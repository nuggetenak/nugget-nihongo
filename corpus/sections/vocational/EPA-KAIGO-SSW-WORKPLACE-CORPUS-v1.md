## 1. THE SOCIO-PRAGMATIC PARADOX: TEXTBOOK JAPANESE VS. WORKPLACE REALITY

Indonesian candidates preparing for work in Japan typically study through standard textbook curricula (e.g., *Minna no Nihongo*, *Marugoto*, or standard JFT-Basic/JLPT N4 prep materials) at vocational training centres (LPK - *Lembaga Pelatihan Kerja*) in Indonesia. These textbooks instill a hyper-formal, sanitized register characterized by strict *desu/masu* polite forms, explicit full-sentence syntactic structures, and textbook situational dialogues (Prihantoro et al. 2024 [EA-26]).

However, empirical fieldwork across Japanese nursing homes (*kaigo roujin hoken shisetsu*), hospitals, and industrial factory floors reveals a massive **pragmatic mismatch** (Noyama 2012 [VS-08]; Pramesti et al. 2019 [VS-29]):

```
[TRADITIONAL LPK / TEXTBOOK PEDAGOGY]
       │
       ▼ (Sanitized Formal Input)
  • Hyper-rigid "Desu / Masu" and formal Keigo
  • Explicit Subject-Verb-Object full sentences
  • Standard Tokyo accent (Hyojungo)
  • Passive listening comprehension in silent rooms
       │
       ▼ (Workplace Immersion Shock)
[ACTUAL JAPANESE WORKPLACE DISCOURSE]
  • Downward Speech Level Shifts (Tameguchi + Empathetic Particulates) for elderly care
  • Ultra-compressed elliptical commands (Chotto kore mite! / Hazushite!) on factory floors
  • Regional dialects (Kansai-ben, Tohoku-ben) & idiosyncratic elderly phonology
  • High ambient noise (75-85 dB) requiring acoustic punch & geminate precision
  • High-stakes non-negotiable protocol: HOU-REN-SO (Bad news delivered first)
```

### The Three Critical Socio-Pragmatic Disconnects for Indonesian L1

1. **The Eldercare Politeness Trap (*The Keigo Paradox*):**
   Textbook training insists that politeness equals formal honorifics (*Teineigo / Kenjougo*). In caregiving (*kaigo*), however, using rigid keigo to an 85-year-old resident with cognitive decline creates an icy emotional distance (*yosoyososhii*), induces anxiety, and often triggers patient non-compliance or agitation. As documented by Pramesti et al. (2019) [VS-29], effective caregiving necessitates **downward speech-level shifts** (*tameguchi* + *ne / yo* affective markers) that simulate familial warmth while preserving core ethical deference. Indonesian caregivers, conditioned by LPK instructors to fear plain forms as "rude", freeze or sound like bureaucratic robots.

2. **The "Sungkan" / ABS (*Asal Bapak Senang*) Cultural Collision in *HOU-REN-SO*:**
   Indonesian communicative culture prioritizes relational harmony (*kerukunan*), deference to seniority (*menghormati atasan*), and indirect conflict avoidance (*sungkan*). When a defect, machine jam, or patient fall occurs, Indonesian workers reflexively delay reporting in hopes of fixing it quietly or soften bad news so as not to upset the supervisor. In Japanese manufacturing and clinical practice, this is catastrophic. The fundamental tenet of *HOU-REN-SO* (報連相) is **"Bad news must be reported first, instantly, and without embellishment"** (*warui houkoku hodo hayaku*).

3. **Phonetic Vulnerability Under Ambient Factory Noise:**
   Indonesian phonology has neither contrastive vowel length (*chouon*) nor phonemic geminate stops (*sokuon* - っ). In an industrial environment with 80 dB machine noise, failure to pronounce a geminate stop turns a safety warning into incomprehensible gibberish. As demonstrated by Husna & Febriyanti (2022) [EA-25], native Japanese speakers' intelligibility ratings drop by over 60% when Indonesian learners shorten geminates or fail to provide the full 1-mora acoustic silence (*heisokukikan*) required for safety commands like *hatte* (貼り付けて) vs. *hate* (果て).

| Interaction Domain | Formal Style (*Teineigo/Keigo*) | Shifted Informal Style (*Tameguchi/Dasshitei*) | Blended / Affective Particle Suffix (*-ne/-yo*) | Primary Communicative Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning Wake-up & Greeting (*Kisho*)** | 18.2% | 42.4% | 39.4% | Establishing emotional security; easing resident out of disorientation. |
| **Bathing & Body Washing (*Nyuuyoku/Seishiki*)** | 12.1% | 51.5% | 36.4% | Mitigating physical vulnerability & nakedness shame (*hazukashisa*). |

| **Handover to Japanese Senior Staff (*Moushiokuri*)** | **94.7%** | 0.0% | 5.3% | Strict clinical accountability and institutional hierarchy. |

#### Key Pragmatic Principle: The Dual-Register Pivot
Indonesian caregivers must master **Register Pivoting**:
- When speaking to **Japanese colleagues, nurses, and supervisors**: 100% formal register (*desu/masu*, *kenjougo*, objective clinical passive).
- When speaking to **Elderly Residents (*Riyousha-san*)**: 70-80% warm shifted register (*tameguchi* + softening morphemes like *~you ka*, *~shiyou ne*, *daijoubu da yo*), shifting back to soft polite (*~kudasai ne*) only for explicit consent requests.

### 2.3 Eldercare Daily Assistance Registers (*Kaigo Kaijo*)

The following table details the operational discourse formulas required for Indonesian caregivers across the five core bodily assistance routines:

#### A. Transfer & Mobility Assistance (*Ijou / Hokou Hojo*)
- **Resident Approach:** 「〇〇さん、車椅子に移りましょうか。足元大丈夫ですか。」 (*OO-san, kurumaisu ni utsurimashou ka. Ashimoto daijoubu desu ka.*)
- **Kinesthetic Cueing (Critical Safety):** 「いち、にの、さんで立ち上がりますよ。ぎゅっと手すりを握ってくださいね。」 (*Ichi, ni no, san de tachiagarimasu yo. Gyutto tesuri o nigitte kudasai ne.*)
- **Checking Equilibrium:** 「ふらつきはないですか。頭クラクラしませんか。」 (*Furatsuki wa nai desu ka. Atama kurakura shimasen ka.*)

#### B. Meal Ingestion & Aspiration Prevention (*Shokuji Kaijo / Enge*)
- **Positioning:** 「少し顎を引いて、ゆっくりもぐもぐしましょうね。」 (*Sukoshi ago o hiite, yukkuri mogumogu shimashou ne.*) — *Chin-tuck position reduces aspiration (*goshoku*) by 80%.*
- **Swallowing Confirmation:** 「ごっくんできましたか。お口の中、見せていただけますか。」 (*Gokkun dekimashita ka. Okuchi no naka, misete itadakemasu ka.*)
- **Choking Emergency Call:** 「看護師さん！〇〇さんがむせ込んでいます！吸引器お願いします！」 (*Kangoshi-san! OO-san ga musekonde imasu! Kyuuinki onegai shimasu!*)

#### C. Excretion Assistance & Dignity Preservation (*Haisetsu / Omutsu Koukan*)
- **De-stigmatizing Suggestion:** 「〇〇さん、少しおトイレに行ってすっきりしましょうか。」 (*OO-san, sukoshi otoire ni itte sukkiri shimashou ka.* — *Never say "Omutsu kaemasu yo" in public!*)
- **Exposing Body Warning:** 「ズボン少し下げますね。寒くないですか。」 (*Zubon sukoshi sagemasu ne. Samukunai desu ka.*)
- **Reassurance:** 「はい、綺麗になりましたよ。気持ちよくなりましたね。」 (*Hai, kirei ni narimashita yo. Kimochiyoku narimashita ne.*)

#### D. Bathing & Hygiene Assistance (*Nyuuyoku / Seishiki*)
- **Water Temperature Verification:** 「お湯加減はいかがですか。熱くないですか。」 (*Oyukagen wa ikaga desu ka. Atsukunai desu ka.*)
- **Step-by-step Body Washing:** 「お背中流しますね。お顔拭きますよ。」 (*Osenaka nagashimasu ne. Okao fukimasu yo.*)
- **Skin Integrity Inspection (Pressure Ulcers):** Internal clinical observation for redness (*sekihan*), skin tear (*hifu hakuri*), or edema (*fushu*).

#### E. Shift Handover Protocol to Senior Nurse (*Moushiokuri*)
- **Standard Clinical Reporting Frame:**
  ```
  「申し送ります。203号室の田中様ですが、14時20分頃、
   車椅子からの立ち上がり時に足元のふらつきが見られ、
   膝をつかれました。外傷および骨折の所見はありませんが、
   血圧が158の92と普段より上昇しています。
   現在ベッドにて安静臥床にしていただいております。
   バイタルサインの再測定を16時に予定しています。以上です。」
  ```

## 3. MANUFACTURING & FACTORY SECTOR (*GEMBA NIHONGO*)

In factory settings (automotive parts, metal pressing, electronics assembly, food processing), Japanese discourse is stripped of ornamental honorifics. It becomes **ultra-dense, elliptical, urgent, and multisensory**.

### 3.1 *Shiteki Koshou* (指差し呼称 - Pointing and Calling) Cognitive Protocol

*Shiteki Koshou* is an industrial ergonomic and behavioral psychology protocol proven to reduce human operational errors by **85%** (JNIOSH standard). For Indonesian workers, it represents an unfamiliar multimodal performance requiring simultaneous **optical fixation, finger pointing, bodily tension, and audible phonetic vocalization**:

```
[EYE: Stare at target] ───► [FINGER: Sharp index point] ───► [EAR: Self-auditory feedback]
                                      │
                                      ▼
                        [VOICE: High-clarity vocalization]
                         "〇〇、ヨシ！" (OO, YOSHI!)
```

#### Core Industrial *Shiteki Koshou* Action Inventory
1. **Machine Start-up:** 「周囲ヨシ！非常停止ボタン解除ヨシ！起動スイッチ、オン！」 (*Shuui yoshi! Hijouteishi botan kaijo yoshi! Kidou suicchi, on!*)
2. **Conveyor Alignment:** 「治具セット、ヨシ！異物混入、ナシ！安全カバー、ヨシ！」 (*Jigu setto, yoshi! Ibutsu konnyuu, nashi! Anzen kabaa, yoshi!*)
3. **Product Inspection:** 「キズ、ナシ！寸法、規格内ヨシ！員数（いんずう）、ヨシ！」 (*Kizu, nashi! Sunpou, kikakunai yoshi! Inzuu, yoshi!*)
4. **Thermal / High Voltage Work:** 「主電源、遮断ヨシ！検電、ゼロボルトヨシ！感電防止、ヨシ！」 (*Shudengen, shadan yoshi! Kenden, zero boruto yoshi! Kanden boushi, yoshi!*)
5. **Forklift Operation:** 「前後左右、安全確認ヨシ！指差呼称、ヨシ！」 (*Zengo sayuu, anzen kakunin yoshi! Shiteki koshou, yoshi!*)

### 3.3 Emergency Imperative Continuum (*Kinkyuu Meirei*)

In dangerous situations, standard polite Japanese disappears. A supervisor shouting an imperative is **not** insulting the worker—they are preventing amputation or death:

```
[LEVEL 1: Standard Polite Directive]
「恐れ入りますが、機械を止めていただけますか。」
         │ (Time available: > 30 seconds)
         ▼
[LEVEL 2: Plain Directive]
「ちょっと機械を止めて！」
         │ (Time available: 5 - 10 seconds)
         ▼
[LEVEL 3: Sharp Workplace Imperative]
「止めろ！」「離れろ！」「触るな！」
         │ (Time available: < 2 seconds — IMMINENT DANGER)
         ▼
[LEVEL 4: Single-Mora Emergency Exclamation]
「危ない！」「ダメ！」「待て！」
```

#### High-Frequency Industrial Imperatives for SRS Mastery:
- **とまれ！** (*Tomare!*) — HALT! DO NOT MOVE A MILLIMETER!
- **はなれろ！** (*Hanarero!*) — GET BACK! MOVE AWAY FROM THE HAZARD!
- **さわるな！** (*Sawaruna!*) — DO NOT TOUCH! HIGH VOLTAGE / CRUSH ZONE!
- **あぶない！** (*Abunai!*) — DANGER! LOOK OUT!
- **しゃがめ！** (*Shagame!*) — DUCK! CRANE OVERHEAD!

## 4. ACOUSTIC & PHONETIC WORKPLACE STRAIN: SOKUON UNDER NOISE

Based on acoustic analysis by Husna & Febriyanti (2022) [EA-25], Indonesian speakers produce Japanese geminate stops (*sokuon* - っ) with an average silent occlusion duration (*heisokukikan*) of only **80–110 ms**, compared to the native Japanese benchmark of **160–220 ms**.

In quiet classrooms, context allows teachers to guess the word. In noisy factories (75–85 dB) or chaotic hospital wards, this **50% duration reduction destroys intelligibility**:

| Target Workplace Word | Native Duration | Indonesian Learner Production | Auditory Misperception by Native Boss | Workplace Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **切って** (*kitte* - Cut it!) | [kitte] (200ms hold) | [kite] (80ms hold) | **来て** (*kite* - Come here!) | Worker approaches dangerous revolving blade instead of cutting power! |
| **待って** (*matte* - Wait!) | [matte] (210ms hold) | [mate] (90ms hold) | Indistinct mumble / *mate* | Co-worker actuates press while partner's hand is inside die! |
| **貼って** (*hatte* - Stick it!) | [hatte] (195ms hold) | [hate] (85ms hold) | **果て** (*hate* - Limit/End) | Inspection label missed; whole shipping container rejected! |
| **取って** (*totte* - Pass it!) | [totte] (205ms hold) | [tote] (95ms hold) | Indistinct acoustic blob | Tools dropped or fumbled during line operations. |

### Pedagogical Prescription in Nugget Nihongo:
SRS audio flashcards for vocational tracks must incorporate **acoustic silence visualizers**:
- Displaying the 1-beat metronome pause: `き` ── [ SILENCE 1 BEAT ] ── `て`!
- Audio playback recorded against synthetic 75 dB ambient background noise to train auditory recognition under ecological workplace acoustic conditions.

## 5. 20 HIGH-STAKES WORKPLACE DIAGNOSTIC SCENARIOS (MICRO-MAPPING)

Below are 20 concrete, granular workplace interaction scenarios mapped directly to SRS card generation:

### Domain A: Caregiving & Healthcare (10 Scenarios)

1. **Morning Apathy Check:**
   - *Resident:* 「今日は何も食べたくないんや...」 (*Kyou wa nanimo tabetakunai n ya...*)
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「食べなければいけませんよ。」 (*Tabenakereba ikemasen yo.* — Authoritarian, triggers refusal).
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「少しお腹の調子が悪いですか。お茶だけでも一口飲みましょうか。」 (*Sukoshi onaka no choushi ga warui desu ka. Ocha dake demo hitokuchi nomimashou ka.*)
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Empathy validation before suggestion; avoidance of modal *nakereba ikenai*.

2. **Dementia Wandering (*Haikai*):**
   - *Resident:* 「家に帰らなきゃ！子供が待ってるの！」 (*Ie ni kaeranakya! Kodomo ga matteru no!*)
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「ここは老人ホームですよ。子供はいません。」 (*Koko wa roujin houmu desu yo. Kodomo wa imasen.* — Confrontational reality-shocking causes panic).
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「お子さんが待っていらっしゃるんですね。今外は暗いので、温かいお茶を飲んでから一緒に探しましょうか。」 (*Okosan ga matte irassharu n desu ne. Ima soto wa kurai node, atatakai ocha o nonde kara issho ni sagashimashou ka.*)
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Validation therapy (*kyoukan*) + redirection (*kinbou henkan*).

3. **Incontinence Incident Discretion:**
   - *Resident:* Accidental bed soiling (*shikkin*).
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「あ、お漏らししましたね！すぐ拭きます！」 (*A, omorashi shimashita ne! — Shames resident*).
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「〇〇さん、少しシーツが濡れてしまいましたね。すぐにお着替えして、さっぱりしましょう！」 (*OO-san, sukoshi shiitsu ga nurete shimaimashita ne. Sugu ni okigae shite, sappari shimashou!*)
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Eliminating accusatory verbs; attributing state to inanimate sheet (*shiitsu ga nurete shimatta*).

4. **Decoding Abdominal Pain (*Kirikiri*):**
   - *Resident:* 「お腹がキリキリ痛むんよ。」
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ Assumes resident is hungry or has gas.
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ Recognizes *kirikiri* = sharp gastric cramp/spasm; checks for coffee-ground emesis or tarry stool, alerts head nurse: 「〇〇様が心窩部にキリキリとした刺痛を訴えられています。」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Onomatopoeia to clinical register translation.

5. **Fall Incident Alert (*Tentou*):**
   - *Resident:* Slipped onto hallway floor.
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ Immediately pulls resident up by arms (causes dislocated shoulder / exacerbates fracture).
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ Tells resident not to move: 「〇〇さん、動かないでそのままでいてくださいね！」; checks consciousness; calls nurse loudly: 「看護師さん！2階廊下で転倒発生です！至急応援お願いします！」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Rapid immobilization + clear acoustic emergency shout.

6. **Medication Refusal (*Fukuyaku Kyohi*):**
   - *Resident:* 「こんな薬、飲みたくない！」
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「薬ですから飲まないと治りませんよ。」
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「苦いのが嫌ですか？それともゼリーで包んで飲みやすくしましょうか？」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Identifying physical obstacle (taste/swallowing difficulty) rather than moral lecturing.

7. **Bathing Resistance Due to Cold:**
   - *Resident:* 「今日はお風呂に入りとうない。」
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「お風呂の日ですから入ってください。」
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「今日は寒いですもんね。じゃあ、足湯だけにするか、温かいタオルでお体拭くだけにしましょうか。」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Offering lower-effort alternative (*seishiki / ashiyu*) preserving autonomy.

8. **Skin Tear Notification to Senior Staff:**
   - *Context:* Found a 2cm skin tear on forearm during transfer.
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ Delayed reporting until end of day or informal mention: 「腕に傷がありました。」
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ Immediate formal report: 「〇〇主任、失礼いたします。佐藤様の右前腕部に約2センチの皮膚剥離を発見いたしました。出血は微量ですが、看護師による処置をお願いできますでしょうか。」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Exact anatomical site + lesion dimension + immediate request for clinical care.

9. **Elderly Dialect Comprehension (Kansai-ben):**
   - *Resident:* 「これ、ほかしといて。」 (*Kore, hokashitoite.* — Kansai for *sutetenoite*).
   - *Learner Error:* ❌ Interprets *hokasu* as *hokan suru* (keep/store safely) and puts trash in the cabinet!
   - *Target Native Care:* ✅ Recognizes *hokasu* = throw away (*suteru*); confirms: 「はい、ゴミ箱に捨てておきますね。」
   - *Pragmatic Focus:* Regional vocabulary mapping preventing severe operational blunders.

10. **Night Shift Resident Hallucination (*Genkaku*):**
    - *Resident:* 「そこに黒い服を着た人が立ってる！」
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ 「誰もいませんよ。気のせいです。」 (Invalidates elderly person, sparks terror).
    - *Target Native Care:* ✅ 「そうですか、怖かったですね。私がついていますから大丈夫ですよ。明かりを少しつけましょうか。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* De-escalating anxiety without confirming false reality.

### Domain B: Manufacturing, Factory & Industrial Safety (10 Scenarios)

11. **Conveyor Jam Emergency Stop:**
    - *Context:* Box caught in drive belt.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Reaches hand into belt while moving to pull box loose.
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Hits emergency E-stop: 「非常停止ヨシ！」 Shouts: 「ライン2停止！ダンボール噛み込み発生！」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Absolute protocol: Stop machine first, shout status second, never reach into live machine.

12. **Chemical Spill Alert (*Yakuhin Kobore*):**
    - *Context:* 5 liters of cleaning solvent spilled on floor.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Grabs ordinary mop and spreads flammable liquid without mask.
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Shouts: 「薬品流出！近づくな！換気ファン起動！吸着マット持ってきてください！」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Protective imperative + collective safety command.

13. **Tool Misplacement Protocol (*Kougu Soushitsu*):**
    - *Context:* 1 torque wrench missing at end of shift check.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Signs inspection sheet hoping it turns up tomorrow.
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Immediate alert: 「班長、工具点検を行いましたが、トルクレンチ1本が所在不明です。製品内部への混入を防ぐため、出荷ラインの一時保留をお願いします。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Zero-defect containment prevents catastrophic product recall.

14. **Overhead Crane Hazard Warning (*Kureen Tsuuka*):**
    - *Context:* Co-worker standing under suspended steel coil.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Polite long sentence: 「〇〇さん、クレーンが上にあるので危ないですよ。」
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Sharp shout: 「危ない！下がるな！クレーン通過中！退避！」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Acoustic compression and Level 3 emergency imperative.

15. **Reporting Dimension Tolerance Exceedance (*Sunpou Gosa*):**
    - *Context:* CNC lathe producing shafts 0.05mm over specification.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Modifies log sheet or waits for batch to complete.
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Stops lathe, reports: 「組長、ロットBの測定値が公差プラス0.05ミリを超過しました。刃具の摩耗が疑われます。補正値の確認をお願いします。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Fact + hypothesized root cause + request for parameter check.

16. **Interpreting Cryptic Elliptical Instructions:**
    - *Supervisor:* 「おい、それチャックしめといて！」 (*Oi, sore chakku shmetoite!*)
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Checks pant zipper!
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Understands *chakku* = lathe machine chuck / workpiece clamp; clamps piece with wrench and confirms: 「チャック締め付け完了ヨシ！」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Factory-specific technical homophones (*chakku* = zipper vs machine chuck).

17. **Refusing Unsafe Operation (*Anzen Saichi*):**
    - *Context:* Asked by senior trainee to operate crane without certification (*mukakunin sagyou*).
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Complies out of fear of angering senior worker (*sungkan*).
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Polite but firm refusal: 「大変申し訳ありません。私には玉掛けの資格がありませんので、安全規則上操作できません。有資格者の方にお願いしていただけますでしょうか。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Citing formal safety law (*anzen kisoku*) removes personal insubordination.

18. **Shift Handover Equipment Anomaly Note:**
    - *Context:* Motor bearing temperature slightly elevated.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Verbal remark: 「ちょっと熱いかも。」
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Clear quantitative log: 「第3ポンプの軸受温度が通常50度のところ、現在68度に上昇しています。異音はありませんが、次シフトでの1時間ごとの温度監視をお願いします。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Quantitative comparison (Baseline vs Current) + explicit handover action item.

19. **Defect Sorting Instruction Clarification:**
    - *Supervisor:* Rapid colloquial instruction: 「キズもんはあっちの赤箱にポイしといて。」 (*Kizumon wa acchi no akabako ni poi shitoite.*)
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Stares blankly or guesses wrong bin.
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Re-confirms: 「はい、傷のある不良品はあちらの赤色の通い箱に隔離廃棄ですね。承知いたしました。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Translating onomatopoeic slang (*poi suru*) into clear procedural action.

20. **Reporting Personal Health Deterioration (*Netchuusho* - Heat Stroke):**
    - *Context:* Dizzy and nauseated in 38°C factory environment.
    - *Learner Error:* ❌ Endures silently until fainting into machinery (*gaman*).
    - *Target Native Workplace:* ✅ Steps away to safe zone, informs partner: 「すみません、めまいと吐き気がして熱中症の初期症状かもしれません。安全な場所で水分補給し、休憩室で休ませていただいてもよろしいでしょうか。」
    - *Pragmatic Focus:* Early bodily awareness preventing severe workplace casualties.

## 6. FOREIGN LANGUAGE WORKPLACE ANXIETY (FLWA) MITIGATION PROTOCOL

Indonesian workers in Japan experience heightened **Foreign Language Workplace Anxiety** (Horwitz et al. 1986 [FLA-01]; Yashima 2002 [VS-05]), driven by three acute psychological stressors:
1. **Fear of Negative Evaluation (*Hyouka Fuan*):** Fear that a linguistic slip will lead to dismissal, reprimand, or visa non-renewal.
2. **Communication Apprehension under Asymmetric Power:** Traditional Indonesian deference to authority figures paralyzes spontaneous speech when facing a strict Japanese foreman (*koujoucho*).
3. **Cognitive Overload in Auditory Noise:** Decoupling sounds from high-tempo machine chatter causes cognitive panic.

### Nugget Nihongo Systematic De-Anxiety Architecture:
- **Proceduralized Sentence Stems (*Kinou-gata Bunkei*):**
  Learners do not compose novel sentences on the fly. They master **5 bulletproof sentence openers** that buy cognitive processing time:
  1. `「恐れ入ります、もう一度ゆっくりお願いできますでしょうか。」` (Clarification)
  2. `「確認させていただきます。[X] という理解でよろしいでしょうか。」` (Echo-confirmation)
  3. `「〇〇についてご相談があるのですが、今1分ほどよろしいでしょうか。」` (Hourenso approach)
  4. `「申し訳ありません、不具合が発生いたしましたので直ちにご報告いたします。」` (Bad news delivery)
  5. `「安全のため、一度作業を止めて確認してもよろしいでしょうか。」` (Safety verification)
- **Overlearning via Fast-Paced SRS (FSRS Latency Targets):**
  Workplace emergency cues must achieve a retrieval latency under **1,200 ms** to qualify as mastered.
